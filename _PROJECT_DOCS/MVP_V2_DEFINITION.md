# MVP V2 Definition

## Purpose

The MVP for ClinIQ 2.0 should define the minimum lovable product: the smallest experience that delivers clear user value, establishes product differentiation, and proves that ClinIQ can be trusted as a daily operational companion.

The MVP should not attempt to solve every workflow at once. It should focus on the highest-value daily needs for the most important users.

---

## Must Have

### 1. Daily Priority View
A clear daily workspace that helps users understand what deserves attention first.

Why it matters:
- This directly addresses the core user need for clarity and action.
- It is the most important daily workflow experience.

### 2. Patient and Case Intelligence View
A structured, actionable view of patient or case progression that goes beyond passive record display.

Why it matters:
- This is the core operational context for students and faculty.
- It turns case data into workflow understanding.

### 3. AI-Guided Next-Step Support
A guidance layer that explains what matters most and suggests likely next actions.

Why it matters:
- This is the clearest differentiator for ClinIQ.
- It reduces cognitive load and helps users act with confidence.

### 4. Operational Exception View
A concise view of bottlenecks, risks, or incomplete work that needs intervention.

Why it matters:
- This supports administrators and operations teams in preventing friction from escalating.

### 5. Role-Aware Experience
The product should present a tailored experience for at least the core roles of student and administrator or faculty.

Why it matters:
- Different users need different guidance.
- A single generic interface will not be trusted for everyday use.

---

## Should Have

### 1. Guided Workflow Nudges
Lightweight prompts that direct attention toward the next most useful action.

Why it matters:
- These make the product feel smart and useful without becoming intrusive.

### 2. Progress and Milestone Awareness
A clearer view of current progress toward important goals, such as student milestones or workflow completion.

Why it matters:
- Progress awareness supports motivation and coordination.

### 3. Cross-Role Summary Views
Views that help faculty or administrators understand team-level status without forcing them into patient-level detail.

Why it matters:
- These make the product more useful for leadership and oversight.

---

## Could Have

### 1. Advanced Forecasting
Predictive views for future workload, follow-up, or bottlenecks.

Why it matters:
- It could create long-term value, but it is not essential for the first lovable experience.

### 2. Expanded Analytics Modules
Broader reporting and trend analysis features.

Why it matters:
- These matter later, once the core daily workflow is established.

### 3. Deeper Integrations
Broader integrations with external systems beyond the initial core flow.

Why it matters:
- Integration breadth should follow product maturity, not precede it.

---

## Won't Build Yet

### 1. Large-scale marketplace features
A marketplace or external app ecosystem is not necessary for the initial product definition.

### 2. Highly customized client-specific modules as part of the first release
Customization should be supported by architecture, but not at the expense of a clear core experience.

### 3. Advanced executive dashboarding as a first-order priority
Leadership value is important, but it should emerge from the core workflow experience rather than be built as a standalone product surface.

### 4. Broad financial or billing orchestration features
These may be valuable later, but they are not the primary value proposition for the first version.

---

## Why This MVP

This MVP is defined by user value, not technical simplicity.

It prioritizes:
- Clarity over completeness
- Daily usefulness over breadth
- Decision support over feature count
- Trust over novelty

The goal is not to build the most features. The goal is to establish a product that users want to return to every day.
