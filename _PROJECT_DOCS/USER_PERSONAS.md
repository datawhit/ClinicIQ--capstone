# User Personas

This document defines the primary user groups that ClinIQ is meant to support based on the current product surface, existing documentation, and the implementation patterns already present in the codebase.

## 1. Dental Student

### Primary responsibilities
- Manage a personal clinical caseload
- Track treatment progress and follow-up tasks
- Maintain visit documentation and patient context
- Monitor graduation-related milestones and pending requirements

### Daily goals
- Keep patients moving through treatment without missing important steps
- Reduce the amount of manual administrative overhead in the day
- Stay organized across multiple patients, procedures, and clinical phases
- Understand what needs attention next

### Frustrations
- Too much information is scattered across systems and notes
- Clinical context is hard to synthesize quickly
- High cognitive load when switching between patient tasks
- Important follow-ups can be missed when the caseload is busy

### Current workflow
- Review a roster of patients and current treatment status
- Open individual patient details to review history and pending items
- Log visits or update treatment information
- Use notes, reminders, and status cues to decide what to do next

### Current tools
- Epic Wisdom as the system of record
- Personal notes and manual task tracking
- Email, reminders, and informal coordination with faculty or staff

### Decision-making responsibilities
- Which patients need attention today
- What follow-up is most urgent
- What treatment tasks should be prioritized
- Whether a patient is progressing appropriately toward completion

### Success metrics
- Fewer missed follow-ups
- Less time spent rewriting or reorganizing information
- More confidence that the caseload is current and actionable
- Better progress toward graduation milestones

### How ClinIQ improves their day
- Surfaces urgent and pending work in a compact, role-relevant view
- Helps turn raw clinical notes into structured, usable information
- Supports faster case review and documentation
- Makes next steps more obvious

### What would make them rely on ClinIQ daily
- A clear daily “what should I do next” experience
- Smart summaries of patient risk, urgency, and readiness
- Guidance that feels useful rather than noisy
- Reliable support for documentation and case progression

## 2. Faculty Member

### Primary responsibilities
- Guide student clinical performance
- Review cases and provide oversight
- Identify students who need support or escalation
- Support quality and consistency in clinical decision-making

### Daily goals
- Understand where students are progressing well and where they are falling behind
- Provide timely and useful feedback
- Reduce the amount of manual follow-up needed to monitor performance

### Frustrations
- Limited visibility into day-to-day student workload and blockers
- Too much effort required to understand student readiness or risk
- Clinical issues are often uncovered too late
- Faculty time is consumed by administrative coordination rather than teaching

### Current workflow
- Review student-related information during clinic flow or administrative time
- Rely on fragmented records and downstream updates
- Intervene when a problem is obvious or escalated
- Coordinate with students and administrators manually

### Current tools
- Epic Wisdom for patient and clinical context
- Email, meetings, and informal communication channels
- Manual review of student activity and status

### Decision-making responsibilities
- Which students need coaching or intervention
- Whether a case is progressing safely and appropriately
- Where workflow friction is affecting education or operations

### Success metrics
- Faster identification of students who need support
- Better quality of oversight with less manual effort
- Improved consistency in student progression and feedback

### How ClinIQ improves their day
- Makes student progress and operational blockers easier to interpret
- Reduces the effort required to see what needs attention
- Offers a higher-level view of student activity without requiring constant manual review

### What would make them rely on ClinIQ daily
- Clear summaries of student readiness, risk, and pending issues
- Actionable recommendations rather than raw data dumps
- A workflow that supports coaching and intervention at the right time

## 3. Clinical Administrator

### Primary responsibilities
- Keep clinic operations running smoothly
- Coordinate patient flow, staffing, and administrative workload
- Support student and faculty needs in the clinic environment

### Daily goals
- Reduce bottlenecks and avoid avoidable disruptions
- Keep scheduling, handoffs, and pending tasks moving
- Maintain visibility into operational pressure points

### Frustrations
- Operational issues are often discovered late
- Information is spread across different tools and workflows
- Manual coordination creates avoidable friction
- It is difficult to understand where the clinic is slowing down

### Current workflow
- Monitor active work and operational issues throughout the day
- Coordinate with faculty, students, and support staff manually
- Follow up on exceptions and pending items
- React to issues after they already affect productivity

### Current tools
- Epic Wisdom for clinical records
- Calendars, spreadsheets, email, and shared administrative systems

### Decision-making responsibilities
- Where to focus attention to improve clinic flow
- Which issues need escalation
- How to rebalance workloads or schedule support

### Success metrics
- Fewer bottlenecks and delays
- Faster resolution of operational exceptions
- Greater visibility into workload and process friction

### How ClinIQ improves their day
- Highlights operational bottlenecks and pending issues
- Gives better visibility into how work is progressing through the clinic
- Helps reduce the need for constant manual status checks

### What would make them rely on ClinIQ daily
- A trusted operational radar for the clinic day
- Clear prioritization of what needs intervention
- Recommendations tied to staffing, scheduling, and workflow flow

## 4. Operations Team

### Primary responsibilities
- Improve clinic operations at a process level
- Monitor service consistency and workflow health
- Identify recurring friction points and improvement opportunities

### Daily goals
- Understand where processes break down
- Find patterns that affect throughput or quality
- Support better decision-making across the broader clinical operation

### Frustrations
- Data is fragmented and not always available in a useful form
- Improvement work relies on manual analysis and reporting
- It is difficult to know whether a problem is one-off or systemic

### Current workflow
- Review reports, exceptions, and trends over time
- Interview stakeholders and look for recurring issues
- Propose process changes based on incomplete or delayed information

### Current tools
- Reporting systems, spreadsheets, and operational summaries
- Manual coordination across departments and stakeholders

### Decision-making responsibilities
- Which process issues deserve intervention
- Where operational design needs to change
- How to prioritize continuous improvement work

### Success metrics
- Better throughput and fewer recurring delays
- More consistent workflows across teams and units
- Faster response to operational issues

### How ClinIQ improves their day
- Turns operational activity into clearer patterns and insights
- Makes it easier to see where the process is creating friction
- Can support proactive rather than reactive management

### What would make them rely on ClinIQ daily
- A dependable view of operational health and trendlines
- Recommendations that help connect daily activity to system-level improvements

## 5. Financial Manager

### Primary responsibilities
- Monitor financial readiness of clinical activity
- Reduce delays and leakage related to documentation, pre-auth, or billing readiness
- Support accurate tracking of treatment progression and associated financial implications

### Daily goals
- Understand where financial friction is emerging
- Identify cases that require follow-up before they become revenue issues
- Improve predictability of clinical revenue operations

### Frustrations
- Financial signals are often delayed or buried in clinical workflows
- Important billing-related conditions can be hard to see in real time
- Manual reconciliation creates avoidable effort

### Current workflow
- Review financial exceptions and case status through existing systems
- Balance clinical progress with billing and administrative readiness
- Follow up on incomplete or risky cases manually

### Current tools
- Epic Wisdom, operational spreadsheets, and financial tracking methods

### Decision-making responsibilities
- Which cases need intervention before financial impact grows
- Where documentation or administrative issues are creating delays
- How to prioritize follow-up work

### Success metrics
- Reduced billing or documentation delays
- Better visibility into risk and readiness
- Greater confidence in the financial state of the clinical workflow

### How ClinIQ improves their day
- Connects operational and clinical signals in a more actionable way
- Helps identify cases that may need intervention earlier
- Improves visibility into tasks that affect downstream financial outcomes

### What would make them rely on ClinIQ daily
- Clear financial-risk summaries and exception handling
- Recommendations focused on likely intervention points
- Integration of operational context with financial awareness

## 6. Executive Leadership

### Primary responsibilities
- Oversee the overall health of the clinical education and operations environment
- Make strategic decisions about resource allocation, growth, and operational quality

### Daily goals
- Understand whether the system is performing well and where it is under strain
- Make better-informed decisions about priorities and investment
- Ensure the organization is improving over time

### Frustrations
- High-level reporting is often delayed or overly abstract
- It is difficult to connect day-to-day activity to strategic outcomes
- Data is not always presented in a form that supports confident decisions

### Current workflow
- Review summaries, reports, and meetings rather than detailed operational activity
- Make decisions based on a mix of formal reporting and informal insight

### Current tools
- Dashboards, reports, and periodic leadership reviews

### Decision-making responsibilities
- Where to invest effort and attention
- Which operational initiatives matter most
- Whether current workflow design is supporting the institution effectively

### Success metrics
- Stronger operational health and stable execution
- Better visibility into trends and opportunities
- More confidence in strategic prioritization

### How ClinIQ improves their day
- Translates operational complexity into clearer executive signals
- Supports quicker understanding of progress, risk, and opportunity
- Helps leadership focus on the highest-leverage issues

### What would make them rely on ClinIQ daily
- Concise, trustworthy summaries of current performance and emerging risks
- Clear indicators that connect daily clinic behavior to institutional outcomes
