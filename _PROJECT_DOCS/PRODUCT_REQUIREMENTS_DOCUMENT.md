# Product Requirements Document

## Executive Summary

ClinIQ 2.0 should evolve from a clinical workflow prototype into an AI Clinical Operations Platform for academic healthcare institutions. The product should help users move from information overload to clear operational action.

The core promise of ClinIQ is simple:
- Epic records information
- ClinIQ transforms information into operational intelligence
- ClinIQ helps students, faculty, administrators, and leaders make better decisions faster

ClinIQ 2.0 should focus on three outcomes:
1. Help students understand what requires attention today
2. Help faculty and administrators identify risk and intervention points earlier
3. Help institutions improve operational performance without adding complexity

---

## Mission

To reduce clinical workflow friction in academic healthcare settings by turning operational data into clear guidance, prioritization, and decision support.

---

## Vision

To become the operating layer for academic clinical operations where institutions can coordinate people, workflows, and decisions more intelligently than they can with records systems alone.

---

## Problem Statement

Academic clinical environments are information-rich but action-poor. Users are often surrounded by data but still struggle to know:
- What matters most today
- What needs intervention first
- What is at risk
- What should happen next

Existing systems such as Epic are essential for recordkeeping, but they do not inherently provide the workflow intelligence needed to reduce cognitive load and improve execution.

ClinIQ exists to close that gap.

---

## Business Opportunity

The opportunity is not to replace electronic health record systems. The opportunity is to create a higher-value layer that improves operations, education, and decision-making.

ClinIQ 2.0 should serve institutions that need:
- Better workflow visibility
- Better prioritization of student and staff activity
- More proactive operational management
- A configurable platform for academic clinical ecosystems

The product has strong strategic value for:
- Dental education programs
- Clinical operations teams
- Faculty oversight workflows
- Administrative coordination
- Leadership reporting and planning

## Core Platform Capabilities

ClinIQ 2.0 should be built around a small set of durable capabilities:

### 1. Daily prioritization
Help users understand what deserves attention first.

### 2. Case intelligence
Provide a clear view of patient or student workflow status and likely next actions.

### 3. AI-guided workflow support
Turn data into explanations, recommendations, and prioritization.

### 4. Operational visibility
Surface bottlenecks, risks, and process friction before they become major issues.

### 5. Role-aware experiences
Deliver tailored views for students, faculty, administrators, and leadership.

### 6. Institutional configurability
Support different workflows, roles, and operating practices across institutions without forcing rebuilds.

---

## Institution Configuration Strategy

ClinIQ should be designed as a configurable platform, not a monolithic product tailored to one institution.

### Configuration layers
- Core platform: shared workflows, intelligence engine, data model, security, and user model
- Institution configuration: local workflows, policies, terminology, roles, and service logic
- Client customization: specific reporting, dashboards, and process tailoring

This architecture is necessary to ensure ClinIQ can grow beyond a single-school proof of concept.

## Product Principles

1. ClinIQ is not an EHR.
2. ClinIQ is an AI Clinical Operations Platform.
3. The product should reduce cognitive load.
4. The product should prioritize action over information.
5. The product should be centered on workflows, not isolated features.
6. The product should be configurable across institutions.
7. The product should be simple enough to be trusted every day.
8. The product should make the next best action obvious.

---

## Non-Functional Requirements

### Reliability
- The product should behave consistently and predictably.
- Important workflow signals should not be lost or ambiguous.

### Security
- Role-based access and data protections should be designed from the outset.
- The platform must support institutional security requirements.

### Scalability
- The architecture should support multiple institutions and varied configurations.
- The platform should not require one-off custom builds for each client.

### Usability
- The experience should feel clear and calm, not overloaded.
- Daily workflows should require minimal explanation.

### Explainability
- AI-generated guidance should be understandable and traceable.
- The system should not fabricate information.

### Extensibility
- New modules should be able to plug into the platform without reworking the core experience.

---

## Risks

### Product risk
The platform may become too feature-heavy and lose focus on the core workflow promise.

### Adoption risk
Users may not trust AI guidance if it feels generic, inaccurate, or overly complex.

### Strategic risk
The product could drift toward becoming a thin layer over existing systems instead of a differentiated platform.

### Configuration risk
A lack of configurable architecture could make the product hard to scale across institutions.

---

## Assumptions

- Users need help prioritizing and acting on clinical information, not just viewing it.
- Institutions value workflow intelligence more than additional dashboards.
- AI can become a meaningful layer of decision support when grounded in trusted data.
- The product will need to coexist with Epic rather than replace it.

---

## Future Expansion

ClinIQ 2.0 should establish the foundation for later expansion into:
- Advanced analytics and forecasting
- Cross-institution benchmarking
- Recommendation engines for operations and staffing
- Expanded integrations with healthcare and education systems
- More role-specific and domain-specific workflow modules
