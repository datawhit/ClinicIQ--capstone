# Design System

This document defines the design language for ClinIQ. It is intentionally conceptual rather than implementation-specific, with a focus on how the product should feel, behave, and communicate.

## Design Principles

### 1. Reduce cognitive load
Every screen should help the user answer: what matters, what to do, and why it matters.

### 2. Prioritize action over information
The experience should make the next step feel obvious.

### 3. Calm over noisy
The interface should feel composed, trustworthy, and clear.

### 4. Guide, don’t overwhelm
Only surface the information needed for the current decision.

### 5. Make AI feel helpful, not theatrical
AI should support the work in a calm, grounded way.

---

## Navigation Philosophy

Navigation should feel like a decision-support system rather than a traditional records interface.

### Core navigation behavior
- Start with the most important question: What needs attention now?
- Let users move quickly from summary to detail
- Preserve a strong sense of context while moving between tasks
- Keep primary actions visible and obvious

### Navigation should support three modes
- Scan: understand what matters most
- Decide: identify what should happen next
- Act: complete the task or workflow step

---

## Card Types

### Summary card
Used for quick status, progress, or attention signals.

### Action card
Used to present a clear next step or recommended intervention.

### Context card
Used to provide supporting facts or recent activity relevant to the current decision.

### Risk card
Used to highlight a concern that needs attention.

### Insight card
Used to display a short AI-generated explanation or recommendation.

---

## Status Indicators

Status indicators should be simple, intuitive, and consistent.

Recommended states:
- Normal
- Pending
- Needs attention
- At risk
- Blocked
- Complete

The system should avoid making users interpret too many visual variants.

---

## Priority Indicators

Priority should communicate urgency and importance without creating alarm.

Recommended priority language:
- Routine
- Soon
- High priority
- Immediate attention

Priority should always be tied to a clear reason, not presented as an abstract label.

---

## Risk Indicators

Risk indicators should be subtle but meaningful.

Recommended language:
- Stable
- Watch
- Elevated risk
- Needs intervention

The product should help users distinguish between routine work and cases requiring active intervention.

---

## AI Interaction Patterns

AI should be embedded in the workflow rather than treated as a separate experience.

### Recommended patterns
- Inline suggestion
- Contextual summary
- Recommendation prompt
- Quiet explanation
- Confidence-aware guidance

AI guidance should feel like an assistant that helps the user think, not a system that demands attention.

---

## Accessibility Principles

The experience should be accessible by default.

### Principles
- Support keyboard navigation throughout
- Ensure contrast and readable typography
- Use clear labels and plain language
- Avoid relying on color alone for meaning
- Make states understandable without visual ambiguity
- Preserve a calm and predictable interaction model

---

## Typography Hierarchy

Typography should support scanning and clarity.

### Hierarchy approach
- Page title: clear, direct, and confident
- Section header: concise and informative
- Body text: readable and calm
- Supporting text: lightweight and secondary
- Interaction labels: explicit and action-oriented

The experience should feel professional without becoming cold or clinical.

---

## Color Philosophy

Color should support clarity and calm rather than visual excitement.

### Conceptual approach
- Use color to communicate state and priority, not decoration
- Reserve stronger color for meaningful attention or risk
- Maintain a clear distinction between neutral information and action-required content
- Keep the experience visually composed and not overly saturated

The design language should feel trustworthy, intelligent, and calm.
